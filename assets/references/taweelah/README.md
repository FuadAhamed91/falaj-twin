## Summary

Taweelah RO is a 200 MIGD (about 909,200 m³/day) seawater reverse-osmosis independent water plant on the Arabian Gulf coast. It sits about 45 km north-east of Abu Dhabi city, in the southern part of the existing Taweelah Power & Water Complex, next to Khalifa Port and KIZAD. ACWA Power developed it and holds 40%; Mubadala and TAQA are the other investors. EWEC buys the water under a 30-year water purchase agreement. Phase 1 started in 2022 and phase 2 in April 2023, and ACWA Power lists commercial operation as Q1 2024.

From the air the plant reads as:

- a **diamond/kite-shaped coastal plot** of about 0.35–0.38 km², almost completely covered in solar panels;
- **eight large flat-roofed process halls with PV-covered roofs**, laid out on a true-north grid, so the grid runs diagonally through the 45°-rotated plot;
- **ground-mounted PV** filling the rest of the plot;
- a **hooked rubble-breakwater seawater intake** on the beach at the plot's northern tip;
- **cooling-water outfall channels** of the old complex, with teal water, along the south-west and south-east sides.

A separate farm of **nine very large round potable-water tanks**, also PV-roofed, lies about 1.1–1.3 km to the ESE.

## Visual reference notes

- **Plot.** A kite/diamond rotated about 45° to the grid. Edges, measured from the OSM polygon:
  - NW edge along the beach: about 710 m, bearing 31°/211°;
  - NE edge along the complex road: about 640 m, bearing 133°;
  - SE edge along a channel: about 470 m, bearing 38°;
  - SW edge along the outfall channel: about 590 m, bearing 110°.
  Corners in the local frame: N tip (−83, +453), E (+389, +16), S (+101, −354), W (−472, −155).
- **Building grid.** The buildings are aligned with **true north**, not with the plot edges. They form a compact column in the centre-north of the plot:
  - 2 halls (north pair) near the intake;
  - a row of 4 halls below them, with the north pair sitting over the 2nd and 3rd;
  - a long E–W hall;
  - 2 lower grey-roofed service buildings;
  - a second long E–W hall at the south.
  This gives **8 large halls with PV roofs** in total. The central long hall has 2 white-roofed ancillary blocks beside it to the east.
- **Roofs.** The large halls are flat and entirely covered with PV blocks in a regular grid, separated by thin white maintenance walkways with a white roof perimeter band. Each of the four middle halls has a lower **white-roofed annex along its south side**, about 23 m deep. The two service buildings have grey roofs covered with rows of rooftop units. The smaller ancillary buildings have bright white membrane roofs.
- **Ground PV.** Long rows running **N–S**, each strip about 18–20 m wide with gaps of about 1 m. Row ends are stepped in a sawtooth along the diagonal plot edges. The gable-shaped row ends suggest **east–west dual-tilt ("tent") racking** with the ridge running N–S, but the racking type is an inference and has not been confirmed. The PV covers the whole W/SW triangle, an E strip between the halls and the NE road, and a band along the S edge.
- **Intake (NW tip).** Two grey **rubble-mound breakwaters** enclose a calm basin of roughly 120 × 150 m:
  - a long **hooked west arm**, about 320 m along its curve and 25–30 m wide at the base, running from the shore at (−162, +328) out to its head at (−315, +598);
  - a **straight east arm** of about 200 m, from (−47, +424) to (−222, +516).
  A thin floating boom crosses the basin mouth. Beige concrete intake and screen structures, about 110 × 70 m, sit on the shore at about (−99, +359).
- **Small accents near the intake.** Two **red-orange round tanks** about 8–9 m across, and a long narrow white-roofed building (about 75 × 20 m) parallel to the NE road.
- **Small white round tanks.** Tanks about 16–18 m across, with domed white roofs, stand next to the central and south halls. Small silver vertical silos and tanks stand near the eastern ancillary blocks.
- **Open basins.** Open rectangular basins with visible greenish water sit immediately west of the north-west hall.
- **Water on three sides.** The Gulf lies to the NW: dark teal-green, lighter over the sand shelf near shore. The **outfall channel of the old complex**, about 60 m wide with teal water and sandy levees, runs along the SW edge and then turns NE along the SE edge. A second channel branch and a sandy spit lie further SE.
- **Neighbours.**
  - NE, across the road: the old **Taweelah A1/A2/B gas-fired power plants and MSF desalination units**, mapped in OSM. They are dense industrial blocks with long multi-bay MSF evaporator rows, white tanks and stacks.
  - About 2 km N: Khalifa Port.
  - About 4 km NE: the EGA aluminium complex.
  - To the S, across the channel: private land planted with trees.
- **Off-site tank farm, about 1.1 km ESE**, centre about (+1118, −632). It holds **6 × Ø105 m and 3 × Ø77 m** flat-roofed tanks, 17.5–17.8 m high, packed in a rounded dark-paved enclosure of about 390 × 300 m. The three smaller tanks sit in the middle row. The roofs are covered in PV in four quadrants divided by a cross-shaped walkway, with white rims. The ESIA confirms the count and sizes. Treat this as the project's tank farm: the location matches "east of the project site" and the dimensions match the ESIA exactly.
- **Kite-shaped ground-PV block**, about 490 × 630 m, centre about (+1124, −1199), just south of the tank farm. It is probably PV on the former construction laydown area, which the ESIA said would receive PV. This is an inference and has not been confirmed.
