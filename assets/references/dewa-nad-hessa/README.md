## Summary

DEWA describes this as a state-of-the-art **132/11 kV digital substation with 150 MVA total capacity**. The Managing Director & CEO inspected it in May 2025. It cost **over AED 93.322 million**, was finished **five months ahead of schedule**, and has **all its facilities integrated into a single building** to minimise the built-up area.

**The exact site is not published.** OpenStreetMap maps several DEWA substations inside Nad Hessa, but none carries the name or voltage of this one, and there is no public exterior photograph of it. The map position is therefore **district-level**, and the 3D model must be an **illustrative generic DEWA 132 kV single-building substation**, not a likeness.

## Visual reference notes (illustrative, generic DEWA 132 kV style)

These notes are based on DEWA's own 2026 illustration and the "single building" statement. They are **not** observations of the Nad Hessa site.

- One **compact, multi-volume building** stepped in plan and height: a taller core of about 4–5 storeys and lower wings of about 2–3 storeys. Flat roofs carry small vents and stacks, with ladders and guard-rails at the roof edges.
- **Facade:** cream/ivory flat panels in a regular grid, with a checkerboard of **tan and taupe accent panels** on one tall corner volume. Openings are few and small.
- **Red vertical pipework** runs down several facades, and there are external steel stair-ladders.
- A **cream perimeter boundary wall** about 3–4 m high surrounds the plot, with a sliding louvred metal gate. The ground is paved or sandy, with a painted kerb.
- Typical context in Nad Hessa: flat suburban/tech-park land with low-rise villas, mid-rise (roughly 6–20 storey) office and residential blocks, wide landscaped roads and highways, and open sand plots.
